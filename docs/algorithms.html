Feature 1 · shared by everything

Finding every nucleus: StarDist

StarDist describes each nucleus as a star-convex polygon: from a centre pixel it predicts the distance to the boundary along fixed directions (rays). Every pixel near a centre proposes its own polygon with a confidence, so each nucleus gets many overlapping proposals; non-maximum suppression (NMS) keeps the most confident one and drops proposals that overlap it too much. Touching nuclei stay separate because each has its own centre.

Few rays draw a crude outline; too low an IoU threshold also deletes the second of two touching nuclei; too high keeps duplicates. PathoAssist uses 32 rays and IoU 0.3 with a probability threshold tuned on validation data (≈0.49).

Tiling large frames

A camera frame is cut into 512 px tiles that overlap by a margin. Each tile is analysed with its margin as context, but only its central part is kept, so a nucleus lying on a seam is found exactly once and always with full context. With no margin, nuclei on the seams are cut in half.

  1. Resize the frame to the model scale, 0.25 µm/px, using the microscope calibration.
  2. For every pixel the network predicts a centre probability and 32 ray distances (INT8 OpenVINO on CPU).
  3. Stitch the tile centres; turn confident pixels into polygons; polygon NMS removes duplicates.
  4. Rasterise the polygons into a label map: one ID per nucleus, used by every scoring feature.

Unmixing the stains: colour deconvolution

Each stain absorbs a characteristic mix of red, green and blue light. By the Beer–Lambert law, optical density OD = −log₁₀(I / I₀) adds up linearly, so a pixel's OD is (amount of haematoxylin × its colour vector) + (amount of DAB × its vector). Inverting that 3 × 3 matrix turns any pixel colour back into stain amounts. Mix stains below and watch the colour the camera would record.

Feature 2 · ER / PR

From counted nuclei to Allred, H-score and status

Feature 3 · Ki-67

Global versus hotspot

Ki-67 uses the same pipeline with one positive/negative cut-off. Global pools every counted field; hotspot takes the fields with the highest index, highest first, until about 500 tumour cells are reached. Bars are fields (height = Ki-67 index, width ∝ cells counted).

Feature 4 · HER2

Reading the membrane, then applying ASCO/CAP

Each nucleus is grown into a cell (watershed on DAB, at most 8 µm). The ring around the nucleus is split into 16 sectors; in each, the membrane is the DAB peak above the field's background. Completeness is the share of stained sectors, intensity their mean OD.

Feature 5 · tumour region & magnification

A fast student taught by a large teacher

The teacher (Phikon-v2, a 300-million-parameter pathology foundation model) is accurate but too slow for live use. A small student (EfficientNet-B0) learns to copy the teacher's soft probabilities on many unlabelled patches. The temperature T softens the teacher's output so the student also learns which wrong classes are "almost right".

illustration

At T = 1 the patch looks like a confident "invasive"; at T = 2 (used in training) the student also sees that it resembles in-situ carcinoma far more than fat or glass. Training data mixes BACH slide patches with BACH microscope photos: a slide-only model got just 33% of photos right.

Feature 5 · mosaic, and de-duplication for every feature

Stitching the sweep into a slide map

Drag the camera window over the slide and press Capture. Each frame is located by phase correlation against the previous one, refined against the map built so far, and pasted in. A frame that overlaps no part of the map starts a new island; when a later frame overlaps two islands, they are merged. For scoring features, a frame overlapping an earlier one by more than 50% is a duplicate and is not counted twice.

Feature 6 · mitosis

Heatmap peaks, a second look, and a score per field area

The detector outputs a heatmap of "mitosis-ness"; local peaks above a threshold are candidates. A verifier network takes a second look at a 96 px crop around each candidate to reject look-alikes (apoptotic cells, dense lymphocytes).

Feature 7 · nuclear pleomorphism

Embeddings of tumour tiles, read by a simple model

Each 40× field is cut into 112 µm tiles; Phikon-v2 turns every tile into 1 024 numbers that describe its look. A case is the average of its tiles. Sixteen principal components and a logistic regression map that average to P(score 1, 2, 3). The plot shows cases in the first two components, with the model's decision regions behind; drag the white case marker.

Feature 8 · tubule formation

Is this space a gland lumen?

Every pale space is a candidate. A real tubule lumen is lined by tumour epithelium: a dense ring of nuclei. Stromal clefts, vessels and fat are not. The classifier measures stain in three rings (0–6, 6–15, 15–25 µm) and the share of the inner ring that is epithelium. Tubule % = epithelium lining accepted lumina ÷ all tumour epithelium.

Audit log (SHA-256 hash chain)

Each entry stores the hash of the one before it. Changing any past entry changes its hash, so the next entry no longer points to it and verification fails. The report prints the final hash as a fingerprint.
